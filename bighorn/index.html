What we build

One call for the whole yard.

Most outdoor projects need dirt work, a solid base, the hard surface, water and a fence. Bighorn handles all of it, so you're not lining up three contractors and hoping they show up in the right order.

Paver patios & walkways

Pavers ride out Reno's freeze–thaw winters better than a single poured slab, because each piece can shift a little without cracking, and a damaged one can be lifted and replaced. Patios, walkways, side yards and fire pit areas, built on a compacted base so they stay flat.

Built for freeze–thaw

The part you never see decides how long it lasts.

Reno winters swing above and below freezing, day after day. Water in the ground freezes, expands and lifts anything sitting on a thin or soft base. That's why pavers sink and slabs crack. A patio that lasts is built in layers, and most of the work is underground.

Interlocking concrete pavers. Thicker units go under anything with tires.

Polymeric sand swept into every joint and set with water. It locks the pavers together and keeps weeds and ants out.

A screeded inch of coarse sand that the pavers are set into. Too much and they wander; too little and they rock.

Crushed aggregate base, compacted in lifts. This is the layer that stops frost heave and settling, and the one cheap jobs skimp on.

On clay or soft soil, a fabric layer keeps the base from pushing down into the dirt below it.

Dug out, graded so water runs away from the house, and compacted before anything goes on top.

A restraint spiked along each open edge holds the whole field in place, so pavers don't creep apart over time.

What areas do you serve?

Reno, Sparks, Sun Valley, Spanish Springs, Washoe Valley, Carson City, Dayton, Fernley, Silver Springs and Fallon. If you're nearby and not on the list, ask.

Pavers or concrete: which should I choose?

Concrete usually costs less up front and suits driveways and simple slabs. Pavers cost more but handle freeze–thaw movement without cracking, and a damaged paver can be lifted and replaced instead of patched. If you're not sure, we'll price both.

When's the best time to build in Northern Nevada?

Spring through fall is the main season for concrete, pavers and sod. Planning, dirt work and some concrete carry on through winter with the right precautions. Booking your walkthrough early gets you a better spot on the schedule.

Do you take small jobs?

Yes. A single walkway, a gate, a shed pad, a sprinkler repair. Tell us what you've got.

Do you work for businesses?

Yes. Commercial work includes office build-outs like glass partition walls, concrete and hardscape, and snow removal for lots and walkways.

How long does a project take?

It depends on size, access and weather. Most residential walkways and patios take days, not months. Your written estimate includes the schedule.
